## CHAPTER ONE - INTRODUCTION

Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System and closest to the sun. Despite being the closest, it is not the hottest - that title is taken by Venus due to its thick atmosphere.

Mercury has a thin exosphere made of atoms blasted off by solar wind and meteoroids, not a real atmosphere. It's composed mainly of oxygen, helium, hydrogen, sodium and potassium.

Day temperatures: up to 800°F (430°C) (700K)
Night temperatures: down to -290°F (-180°C) (100K)

**Ability to support life:** These conditions explain why it's regarded as unconducive to life.

**Size and distance:**
- Radius: 1,516 miles (2,440 km) - just wider than 1/3 of Earth
- Average distance: 36 million miles (58 million km) from Sun
- Sunlight takes 3.2 minutes to travel to Mercury
- Sun appears 3x larger and 7x brighter than from Earth

**Formation and structure:**
Formed 4.5 billion years ago when gravity pulled swirling gas and dust together. Has a central core, solid crust and rocky mantle, no moons or rings. Second densest planet after Earth with large metallic core 1,289 miles (2,074 km) - 85% of its radius.

**Time and Periods:**
One Mercury Solar day (full day-night cycle) equals 176 Earth days (4,224 hours).

**Orbits and Rotation:**
Orbits as close as 29M miles (47M km) and as far as 43M miles (70M km). Orbits Sun every 88 Earth days at 29 miles/sec (47 km/s) - fastest planet. Rotates every 59 Earth days. Axis tilted only 2 degrees - no seasons.

## CHAPTER TWO - MISSIONS TO PLANET MERCURY

**NASA's MESSENGER**
Launched August 3, 2004, inserted March 17, 2011 - first robotic probe to orbit Mercury.

**Before MESSENGER**
They told us Mercury was a dead rock with no water. That was Mariner 10 in 1974. Half a planet mapped, no ice, case closed.

Then MESSENGER orbited 2011-2015 and found it. Radar-bright ice in craters that never see sunlight. Up to 26 metres thick in Prokofiev. At 100 kelvin, -173 Celsius.

**They told us the ice was pure. It wasn't.**
The brightest ice is covered by a dark layer. Tens of centimeters thick. Silicates or hydrocarbons, delivered by comets, or cooked by radiation. Sharp edges - means it's young.

**They did not tell us what was under the dark.**

### The Prokofiev's Shadow
On January 7, 2013, NASA's MESSENGER with its Wide Angle Camera peered into the dark pits of Mercury's North Pole and captured water ice on the surface.

Permanently shadowed in Prokofiev crater, the largest crater in Mercury's North Polar Region to host radar-bright material. 112 kilometers (70 miles) diameter.

Mechanism: Mercury has virtually no axial tilt. Crater rims block sunlight, creating permanent shadow at -280°F (-173°C) (100K), allowing water ice to remain stable for billions of years.

### The prospect about the near future
**The First Ugandan to Planet Mercury**

I was hired to ignore the dark, mine the pure ice below, make propellant, and keep the lights on for Tendik Outpost.

On Sol 87, my drill hit 9m and stopped making sense.

The spectrometer spiked at 16, then 28, then a forest of peaks - methane, ethylene, something with a benzene ring that shouldn't survive at 100 Kelvin.

In my ear, the delay light was red. Thirteen minutes to Earth, thirteen back. I would be alone with the decision for 26 minutes.

I turned off the heater. The black layer stopped sublimating. It started to hold heat. Thermal curve inverted.

Ice was supposed to cool when heated - energy goes to phase change. That's why the drill was a heater. You sublimate, you capture, you make water.

This didn't cool. Bit climbed: 120K, 140K, 170K. The dark was not reflecting heat. It was drinking it.

I switched to visual. Bore-camera had been blinded by ice glare for two months. Now at 9m, it saw black. Not rock, not regolith. Matte, granular black like soot pressed into snow. And in the black, pores. Little bubbles, frozen mid-pop.

Cometary. MESSENGER said the dark could be hydrocarbons delivered with ice - but those were supposed to be a thin lag, centimetres left when ice sublimated away.

This was a metre thick. And it was structured.

At 180 Kelvin, the pores started to move. A soft hiss hit the suit microphone - not through radio, through the ground. Conduction. Ice below the dark was finally warming, vapour pushing up through the black like breath through a cloth.

Mass spec screamed again. Not water. Water was 18. This was 32, 44 - heavier, stickier. Methanol? Carbon dioxide trapped since the impact that made Prokofiev?

Then torque dropped to zero. It wasn't drilling anymore. It was falling.

The ice beneath the dark layer was gone. There was a void.

My boot lights flickered. In permanent shadow, you don't have a horizon. You have crater walls lit by scattered light, and a hole you just opened to something that had not seen photons in a billion years.

From the hole, vapour rose. And it did not freeze. Vapour in vacuum should snow out. At 100K, water molecules stick to first cold surface and form frost instantly. This vapor didn't. It rose two metres, then stopped as if hitting a ceiling. Spread sideways, forming a flat, faint haze at chest height.

I knelt. The hole was not a void. It was a chamber. The drill had punched through a roof.

Nine metres down, walls changed from dirty ice to something smooth. A melt lens. Around edge, black material was layered like varnish. Year after year, impact after impact, organics settling, radiation baking it.

At the bottom - six metres down - was ice that was not radar-bright. It was clear, transparent, old ice. And inside it, trapped like insects in amber, were white grains.

Not bubbles. Grains that did not sublimate. Grains that were reflecting my light back.

BepiColombo missed it because MESSENGER told it to look for white craters. Neutron spectrometer averaged over kilometers. Radar saw bright ice, or dark cover. It could not see a basement. This was a basement, a cold trap under a cold trap. The dark lag was not a tomb. It was a lid.

I had not found water. I had found a vault that had kept something from the early solar system at 100 Kelvin for four billion years.

### The Vault
The lid holds. The vault at 100K is still at 100K. I sealed the hole on Sol 88. Vapor flat at 2m. The white grains are still there and I am not mining it. Protocol says wait for 26 minutes for Earth. I am waiting - Baron, Sol 87, Tendik.
